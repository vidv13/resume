AKS vs
App Service

Revisited under the MLZ constraint. The headline conclusion survives, but the reasoning changed: private networking requirements erase most of App Service's low-end cost advantage, which pulls the crossover point down to roughly two or three apps.

What changed: the earlier comparison priced App Service as if you could run Basic or Standard plans with public endpoints. Inside an SCCA boundary you can't — private endpoints and VNet integration require Premium tier at minimum, and each app needs its own private endpoint. Premium v4 is now GA (Microsoft reports over 50% better performance than v3 for the P1–P3 range), which improves price-performance but doesn't change the per-plan floor. Exact Pv4 list rates vary by region — treat the App Service figures here as directional and confirm in the calculator.

Each app assumes dev, staging, and prod instances. AKS cost is mostly fixed platform overhead; App Service scales close to linearly because each app needs plan capacity and its own private endpoint. Drag to find your crossover.

Cost Crossover by App Count

AKS carries high fixed cost and near-zero marginal cost. App Service inside MLZ carries a Premium-tier floor per plan plus a private endpoint per app, so it climbs steadily. They cross at roughly 2–3 apps.

⎈ AKS — what you gain
✓Fixed platform cost. App number ten costs almost nothing more than app number five. Scales economically with your portfolio.
✓Any language, any runtime. Your C#, Python, React, and Angular apps all deploy through one identical mechanism.
✓Pipeline-only deployment is enforceable by network topology. A private API server plus namespace RBAC makes manual deploys physically impossible, not merely discouraged.
✓One private endpoint set for shared services, regardless of app count. App Service needs one per app.
✓Staging is a namespace, not a separate paid resource — which removes the budget incentive to skimp on it.
—Higher fixed cost. You pay for the platform before the first app ships.
—More surface area to own: node patching, ingress config, firewall rule requests.
—Longer setup. Realistically 4–8 weeks inside MLZ, mostly spent on private networking.
☁ App Service — what you gain
✓Much faster to first deploy. Days rather than weeks, even accounting for VNet integration and private endpoint setup.
✓Deployment slots give you blue/green and instant rollback without writing any Helm.
✓Premium v4 is now GA with materially better price-performance than v3 — Microsoft reports over 50% higher throughput on the P1–P3 range.
✓Far less to operate. No node pools, no ingress controller, no cert-manager, no cluster upgrades.
✓Still cheaper at one or two apps, and a legitimate answer if your portfolio genuinely stays that small.
—Private endpoints force Premium tier everywhere, including dev — the cheap-non-prod trick isn't available.
—Cost scales with app count. Ten apps across three environments is a lot of plan capacity and thirty-ish private endpoints.
—Harder to enforce pipeline-only deploys. Portal and CLI publish paths always exist, so MinimumCD discipline relies on culture rather than topology.

The Verdict

At 5 apps, AKS comes out —, and the gap widens with every app you add. But the honest headline is that MLZ shifted the crossover point down, not the conclusion. Private networking is expensive for App Service in a way it isn't for AKS, because the cost attaches per app rather than per platform.

The MinimumCD argument is the stronger one. In App Service, anyone with publish rights can push from a laptop or the portal, and staging environments get skimped because each one is a separately billed resource. Under AKS with a private API server, the pipeline is the only door — enforced by the network, not by a policy document nobody reads.

Recommendation: AKS, given you expect five or more apps and you're building for CD discipline from the start. If you'd rather ship something in two weeks than build a platform in eight, App Service Premium with private endpoints is a defensible interim step, and the platform repo, Helm charts, and pipeline templates from this architecture largely carry over when you migrate.

Figures are directional US-region list prices. App Service Premium v4 rates vary by region and Pv4 isn't available everywhere yet — confirm in the Azure Pricing Calculator against your actual agreement before committing.
